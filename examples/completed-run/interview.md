# Adaptive interview

## Question 1 — direct thesis

**Question:** What do managers misunderstand about decision logs?

**Human answer:** They sell them as good documentation. My team cared when I described them as a way to stop paying twice for the same decision.

**Obtained:** Distinctive “pay twice” thesis and creator language. **Missing:** A scene and causal detail. **Next move:** Concrete example.

## Question 2 — concrete example

**Question:** Walk me through one moment when the team paid twice.

**Human answer:** We reopened whether invoices should be generated synchronously. Six people spent most of a meeting reconstructing constraints we had already discussed. The original choice held. The waste was not changing our minds; it was rebuilding the path to the same answer.

**Obtained:** Scene, consequence, and mechanism. **Missing:** Why the intervention worked. **Next move:** Causal specificity.

## Question 3 — strongest objection

**Question:** Why isn't a log just more process that busy teams will abandon?

**Human answer:** It is if it becomes minutes. Ours had four fields: decision, reason, owner, revisit trigger. Writing it took under five minutes. The revisit trigger mattered because it made disagreement conditional rather than forbidden.

**Obtained:** Objection, minimum practice, and differentiator. **Missing:** Reader payoff and boundary. **Next move:** Practical takeaway.

## Question 4 — practical takeaway

**Question:** What should a team try, and when should they not use it?

**Human answer:** Log choices that are expensive to reconstruct, not every choice. Try it for two weeks. If nobody reopens a logged decision or uses its trigger, stop. The goal is lower decision latency, not a full archive.

**Obtained:** Action, test, boundary, and payoff. **Missing:** Nothing blocking; numerical estimate remains caveated. **Next move:** Stop on coverage and brief.
